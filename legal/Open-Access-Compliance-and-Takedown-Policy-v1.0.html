1. Purpose and Scope of this Policy

This Policy sets out, openly and without reservation, the basis on which STM Digital Library (“the Platform”) makes scholarly content available to its users. It is published so that any author, journal, publisher, repository, funder or institution can see precisely what the Platform does with third-party material, what it charges for, what it does not claim, and how it can be required to stop.

The Company’s position is stated plainly rather than buried: the Platform aggregates Open Access scholarly material that is already lawfully and freely available to the public; it does not present that material as its own; it attributes every item to the journal and publisher that produced it; it charges its subscribers for the software and services it has built, not for the underlying scholarship; and it will remove any item on the request of the rights holder, without requiring that rights holder to litigate, prove infringement, or explain themselves at length.

This Policy applies to all content made accessible through the Platform and to all subscribers, institutional users and visitors. It supplements, and must be read together with, the Platform’s Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, Legal Disclaimer and Content Sources & Licensing statement. Where a conflict arises between this Policy and any of those documents on the subject of third-party content rights, this Policy prevails.

3. What the Platform Is — and What It Is Not

3.1 What the Platform is

The Platform is a software and technology service. Its subject matter is the problem of finding, filtering, organising and reading scholarly material at scale. What the Company has built and owns is the software: the harvesting and de-duplication pipeline, the normalised metadata index, the department- and subject-level classification, the cascading discovery filters, the secure in-application reader, the institutional access and entitlement management system, the usage analytics, and the administrative tooling that keeps all of it running.

3.2 What the Platform is not

The Platform is not a publisher of third-party works. It does not commission, peer-review, edit, typeset, brand or assert editorial control over third-party scholarship. It does not sell third-party articles. It does not represent that it holds a licence from every rights holder whose Open Access work is discoverable through it, and it has never done so. It is not the source of record for any third-party work; the source of record is, and remains, the publisher’s own site of publication, identified on the Platform by DOI wherever a DOI exists.

The distinction that matters. A subscriber who cancels their subscription loses access to the Company’s software. They do not lose access to the underlying Open Access scholarship, which remains freely available to them — as it was before, and as it will be after — at the publisher’s own website. The Platform’s subscription has never been the thing standing between a reader and an Open Access article.

5. How Open Access Content Is Sourced

5.1 Sources

Harvested content is obtained through the public application programming interfaces of established scholarly infrastructure services, namely OpenAlex, the Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) and arXiv. The Company does not scrape publisher websites, does not circumvent paywalls, does not use credentials belonging to any institution or individual to obtain content, and does not obtain content from unauthorised or “shadow” repositories.

5.2 Open Access filtering at source

Harvesting queries are constrained at the API level to works that are marked as Open Access. In the case of OpenAlex, the query filter applied is open_access.is_oa:true together with has_doi:true, so that only Open Access works carrying a persistent identifier enter the pipeline. Works that are not marked Open Access at source are never requested and never enter the index.

5.3 Full text is streamed, not stored

This is a material feature of the Platform’s design and of its legal position. Where an Open Access full text is made available to a reader, the Platform does not hold a copy of it. The file is requested from the originating host at the moment the reader opens it, and streamed through to the reader’s browser. Nothing is retained afterwards. The Platform’s storage contains bibliographic metadata and a pointer; it does not contain a library of third-party PDFs.

Consequently, the Platform’s handling of third-party full text is transient and incidental storage for the purpose of providing access, and not the making or distribution of copies. This is directly relevant to Section 10.1 below.

5.4 Restricted host allow-list

Full text is streamed only from an explicit allow-list of Open Access-native hosts — including arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, BioMed Central, PLOS, Frontiers, eLife, PeerJ, eScholarship, F1000Research, Copernicus, SciELO and J-STAGE. Any link that resolves to a host outside that allow-list is not streamed. Records whose full text cannot be served in this way are retained as metadata-only records under Section 4, with the reader directed to the publisher.

6. Attribution and Non-Misrepresentation

The Company undertakes the following, and these undertakings are enforceable against it by any rights holder relying on this Policy.

  1. Every record carries its origin. For each third-party item the Platform stores and displays the author names, the journal or source title, the ISSN where available, the publishing organisation, the volume, issue, pages and year of publication, and the DOI.
  2. The publisher’s name is shown, not the Company’s. Third-party content is displayed under the name of the journal and publisher that produced it. The Company does not substitute its own name, logo or imprint for that of the originating publisher, and does not present third-party scholarship as having been published by the Company or any of its divisions.
  3. No re-branding, no watermarking, no alteration. Third-party full text is served exactly as it is received from the originating host. The Company does not overlay its branding on it, does not re-typeset it, and does not create derivative versions of it.
  4. Attribution is not conditional on subscription. Attribution metadata is shown to every visitor, including those with no subscription and those who are not signed in.
  5. The DOI is a route out, not a dead end. Where a DOI exists it is displayed and is resolvable, so that a reader can always reach the publisher’s own site of publication directly.

7. No Claim of Ownership

The Company does not claim, and expressly disclaims, any ownership of, or exclusive right in, any third-party content made available through or referenced on the Platform. All such content remains the intellectual property of its respective authors, publishers, licensors and rights holders. Inclusion of an item on the Platform does not imply ownership, endorsement, affiliation, sponsorship or any exclusive right on the part of the Company. Any right not expressly granted to the Company by a rights holder is reserved to that rights holder.

Nothing on the Platform, and nothing in the Company’s marketing, should be read as a claim that the Company owns the scholarly corpus it indexes. Where any such impression has been created, whether by wording, layout or emphasis, the Company will correct it on being notified.

8. Basis of Charging: Fees Are for the Software, Not the Content

8.1 The consideration

Subscription and licence fees payable to the Company are consideration for access to the Company’s software and services. They are not, and have never been presented as, the purchase price of any third-party article, chapter or dataset.

What the subscriber is paying for is:

  • a normalised, de-duplicated and continuously maintained index across a very large number of sources, which the subscriber would otherwise have to assemble and maintain themselves;
  • subject and departmental classification, and the cascading journal → year → volume → issue discovery filters built on top of it;
  • a secure in-application reader, including streaming of large documents;
  • institutional access management — entitlements by department and content type, user provisioning, suspension, and IP-based and credential-based authentication;
  • usage analytics, reading history, favourites and continuity of reading across devices;
  • administrative tooling for librarians, and support, hosting, uptime and maintenance.

8.2 What the fee is not

  • It is not a fee for the right to read any Open Access article. That right is granted by the article’s own licence, directly to the reader, by the rights holder — not by the Company.
  • It does not create exclusivity. The Company does not restrict, and cannot restrict, anyone’s access to Open Access content at its original source.
  • It does not place Open Access content behind a paywall. Content that is Open Access at source remains Open Access at source, unaffected by anything the Company does.
  • It is not shared with, and does not create any revenue-sharing obligation to, third-party rights holders in respect of harvested Open Access content — because it is not a fee for that content.

8.3 Non-commercial licences

The Company recognises that the distinction drawn in this Section is not by itself a complete answer where a work is licensed on non-commercial terms. Its commitments in that regard are set out in Section 9.3 and are operational, not merely declaratory.

9. Licence Compliance Framework

9.1 Principle

Open Access does not mean unconditional. Each Open Access work carries a licence, and the licence governs what may be done with the work. The Company’s obligation is to honour the licence attaching to each item, not merely the fact that the item is Open Access.

9.2 Treatment by licence family

CC BY-SA Indexed and streamed with attribution. The Platform creates no adaptations, so the share-alike obligation is not engaged.
CC BY-ND Indexed and streamed unmodified, with attribution. No derivative, abridged, re-typeset or re-formatted version is created or served.
CC BY-NC and CC BY-NC-ND / NC-SA Excluded from paid tiers. Not to be made available as part of any subscription or fee-bearing service, unless the rights holder has expressly permitted it in writing. See Section 9.3.

Bespoke or unclear licence Treated as restricted. Held as a metadata-only record under Section 4 until the licence is positively established.

9.3 The non-commercial commitment

Content carrying a non-commercial restriction will not be included within a paid service of the Company unless the rights holder has expressly permitted that use. Where such content is identified within a paid tier, it will be removed from that tier promptly on identification, whether the identification is made by the Company or is notified by a third party.

9.4 Derivative works

The Company does not create derivative works from third-party content. It does not translate, summarise for republication, abridge, re-typeset, or generate new works from third-party full text. Machine processing performed by the Company is confined to indexing, classification and de-duplication, and its output is bibliographic metadata rather than a substitute for the work.

9.5 Current implementation status — stated candidly

The Company records that, as at the date of this version, the Platform’s ingestion pipeline captures bibliographic metadata but does not yet capture and store the licence identifier of each harvested item. In consequence, the commitments in Sections 9.2 and 9.3 are presently given effect through source-level and host-level controls (Sections 5.2 and 5.4) and through responsive removal on notice (Section 11), rather than through per-item licence verification.

The Company undertakes to implement per-item licence capture and to apply the classification in the table at Section 9.2 automatically at the point of ingestion. Until that is completed, any rights holder who considers that a work of theirs carrying a non-commercial or other restrictive licence has been included in a paid tier is invited to notify the Company under Section 11, and the item will be removed without the Company requiring any further proof.

This paragraph is included deliberately. A policy that overstates the maturity of the controls behind it is worse than no policy, because the overstatement is itself actionable. The Company prefers to state the position accurately and to close the gap.

10. Legal Framework Relied Upon

The following is a statement of the framework within which the Company operates. It is a summary for the reader’s orientation and is subject to confirmation by counsel; it is not a legal opinion.

10.1 The Copyright Act, 1957

Section 52 of the Copyright Act, 1957 sets out acts that do not constitute infringement. Section 52(1)(c) addresses transient or incidental storage of a work for the purpose of providing electronic links, access or integration, where such access has not been expressly prohibited by the rights holder, and where the person responsible is not aware and has no reasonable ground for believing that the stored copy is infringing. Given the design described in Section 5.3 — streaming from the originating Open Access host without retention — the Company’s handling of third-party full text is intended to fall within this provision.

The proviso to Section 52(1)(c) prescribes the mechanism that follows a complaint: on receipt of a written complaint from the owner of copyright, the person responsible for the storage must refrain from facilitating access for twenty-one days, or until receipt of an order of a competent court. If no such order is received within that period, access may be resumed. The procedural requirements for such a complaint are set out in the Copyright Rules, 2013. The Company’s takedown procedure at Section 11 is built around this mechanism, and is deliberately more generous to the complainant than the statute requires.

10.2 The Information Technology Act, 2000

The Company operates the Platform as an intermediary within the meaning of Section 2(1)(w) of the Information Technology Act, 2000, insofar as it provides access to third-party material. Section 79 affords an intermediary exemption from liability in respect of third-party information, subject to the conditions stated in that section — including that the intermediary observes due diligence, does not initiate or select the receiver of the transmission or select or modify the information, and expeditiously removes or disables access to material on receiving actual knowledge, or on being notified by the appropriate Government or its agency.

The Company accordingly does not modify third-party content (Section 6, item 3), does not select recipients, and maintains the notice-and-action machinery at Section 11.

10.3 The Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code Rules, 2021

The Company observes the due diligence obligations applicable to intermediaries under these Rules, including publication of its rules, privacy policy and user agreement; appointment of a Grievance Officer whose name and contact details are published (Section 14); acknowledgement of a complaint within twenty-four hours and its disposal within fifteen days of receipt; and removal or disabling of access within thirty-six hours of actual knowledge in the form of a court order or a notification by the appropriate Government or its agency. The Company is not a significant social media intermediary and the additional obligations applicable to such intermediaries are not engaged.

10.4 The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

Personal data processed by the Platform — including subscriber account data, institutional user records and usage logs — is handled in accordance with the Company’s Privacy Policy and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. This Policy is concerned with content rights and does not displace the Privacy Policy.

10.5 Complainants outside India

The Company will accept and act on a notice from a rights holder situated in any jurisdiction, and does not require the complainant to establish a connection with India. A notice framed in the form customary in the complainant’s own jurisdiction — including a notice in the form contemplated by 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3) — will be accepted and acted upon under Section 11, provided it contains the information at Section 11.3.

11. Rights-Holder Objection and Takedown Policy

11.1 The commitment, stated first

If you are the author, publisher, or rights holder of a work that appears on this Platform, and you do not want it there, tell us and we will take it down. You do not need to prove infringement. You do not need to instruct a lawyer. You do not need to explain your reasons. You do not need to threaten proceedings. One email to info@celnet.in, or one completed form, is enough.

11.2 Who may object

A request may be made by: the author of the work; the publisher, journal or learned society in which it appeared; the institution or repository that hosts it; a funder with rights in it; an assignee or exclusive licensee; or any person duly authorised to act on behalf of any of the foregoing. Where the request is made by a representative, Annexure B should accompany it.

11.3 What a request should contain

To allow the Company to identify the material and act on it, a request should state:

  1. the name, organisation, postal address, email address and telephone number of the person making the request;
  2. the capacity in which the request is made (author, publisher, assignee, authorised representative, or otherwise);
  3. sufficient particulars to identify the material — the title, the DOI where one exists, and the URL on the Platform where the material appears. A list may be annexed where several items are concerned, and a journal or ISSN may be given instead where the request concerns an entire title;
  4. the relief sought — removal of full-text access, removal of the record in its entirety, correction of attribution, or a standing exclusion of the title going forward;
  5. a statement that the requester believes in good faith that they are entitled to make the request; and
  6. the requester’s signature, whether physical or electronic.

A reason for the request is welcome but is not required. The Company will not decline to act on a request merely because no reason has been given, nor because the requester has not asserted infringement.

11.6 Scope of removal

Unless the requester specifies otherwise, removal means that full-text access through the Platform ceases and the record is delisted from search and browse. Where the requester asks for complete erasure, the record is deleted. Where the requester asks only that attribution be corrected, the record is corrected and retained. The requester chooses; the Company does not choose for them.

11.7 Standing exclusion and no re-acquisition

A rights holder may ask to be excluded from the Platform permanently and in advance, without identifying particular items. On such a request the journal, ISSN or publisher is placed on an exclusion list which is applied to every subsequent harvest, so that the material does not reappear. This is available on request at any time and requires no reason.

11.8 Restoration

Material removed under this Section is restored only if the rights holder who requested its removal asks for it to be restored, or if a person entitled to do so establishes that the removal request was made without authority. The procedure is at Annexure C.

11.9 No retaliation, no cost

The Company will not charge any fee for processing a request, will not require a requester to enter into any agreement as a condition of removal, and will not treat the making of a request as a reason to decline any future dealing with the requester.

12. Publisher Partnership and Standing Opt-Out

A publisher whose material is discoverable through the Platform has, at all times, three options, and may move between them freely:

  1. Take no action. The material continues to be indexed with attribution under this Policy.
  2. Opt out. The material is removed and the title is placed on the exclusion list under Section 11.7. No reason is required and no cost is incurred.
  3. Partner. The publisher takes a login to the Platform’s publisher portal, from which it can submit and manage its own content, control what is published, correct metadata, see readership analytics for its own titles, and withdraw any item at any time without going through the Company.

The partnership option is offered on its merits. It is not a condition of removal, and a publisher who declines it is not thereby disadvantaged in any way in the handling of a removal request.

13. Records, Audit Trail and Transparency Reporting

The Company maintains, for each item of third-party content, a record of the source from which it was acquired, the date of acquisition, the classification applied under Section 4, and the identity of the originating journal and publisher. For each request received under Section 11, the Company maintains a record of the request, the date of receipt, the action taken, the date of that action and the identity of the officer who took it. These records are retained for not less than five years.

The Company intends to publish a periodic transparency report stating the number of removal requests received, the number acted upon, and the median time to action.

Standing instructions

  • Do not ask a requester to prove infringement, to produce a chain of title, or to instruct a lawyer, as a condition of acting.
  • Do not offer, suggest or imply that a partnership, subscription or commercial arrangement would avoid the need for removal.
  • Do not delay a removal pending a commercial discussion of any kind.
  • Where the request is ambiguous as to scope, apply the broader removal and tell the requester what was done, rather than applying the narrower removal and waiting.
  • Escalate to a Director immediately where the request alleges bad faith, threatens proceedings, or comes from a regulator.

Register. The takedown register records, for each request: reference number; date received; requester and capacity; material concerned; relief sought; interim suspension date; decision; decision date; officer; and date closed. It is retained for not less than five years and is the evidence that this Policy is operated in practice rather than merely published.
